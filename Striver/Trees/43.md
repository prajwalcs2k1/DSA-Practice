# Recover Binary Search Tree (BST)

## Problem Statement

You are given the root of a Binary Search Tree (BST) in which exactly two nodes were swapped by mistake.

Recover the BST without changing its structure.

---

# Example

Corrupted BST:

```text id="u2c4y8"
                        6
                      /   \
                     5     12
                   /  \   /  \
                  4    7 10  14
                      /     /   
                     8     13
```

Correct BST should be:

```text id="j8m5q1"
                        8
                      /   \
                     5     12
                   /  \   /  \
                  4    7 10  14
                      /     /   
                     6     13
```

---

# Key Observation

### Inorder Traversal of a BST is always sorted

For a valid BST:

```text id="n4k7x2"
4 5 6 7 8 10 12 13 14
```

But after swapping two nodes:

```text id="r6p3d9"
4 5 8 7 6 10 12 13 14
```

Notice the violations:

```text id="w1f8c4"
8 > 7
7 > 6
```

These violations help us detect the swapped nodes.

---

# Types of Swaps

There are two possible cases.

---

# 1. Adjacent Swapped Nodes

Example:

Original inorder:

```text id="m9q2v6"
1 2 3 4 5
```

After swapping adjacent nodes `3` and `4`:

```text id="b7t5k1"
1 2 4 3 5
```

Only ONE violation occurs:

```text id="x3p6r8"
4 > 3
```

Detected nodes:

```text id="l5n9c2"
first  = 4
second = 3
```

---

# 2. Non-Adjacent Swapped Nodes

Example:

Original inorder:

```text id="d4y8w7"
1 2 3 4 5
```

After swapping `2` and `5`:

```text id="h6v1q3"
1 5 3 4 2
```

Two violations occur:

```text id="k8r4m9"
5 > 3
4 > 2
```

Detection process:

### First violation

```text id="p2x7c5"
first  = 5
second = 3
```

### Second violation

Update only second:

```text id="s9w3n6"
second = 2
```

Final swapped nodes:

```text id="g4t8k1"
5 and 2
```

---

# Approach

## Step 1: Perform inorder traversal

Maintain a pointer called `parent` (or `prev`) which stores the previously visited node in inorder traversal.

---

## Step 2: Detect violations

If:

```cpp id="e7m2q5"
current->data < prev->data
```

then BST property is violated.

---

## Step 3: Store swapped nodes

### First violation

```cpp id="t4n8p1"
first = prev;
second = current;
```

### Second violation

```cpp id="v6k3r9"
second = current;
```

---

## Step 4: Swap values back

```cpp id="z1x5c8"
swap(first->data, second->data);
```

BST gets recovered.

---

# Dry Run

Corrupted inorder:

```text id="y3p7m2"
4 5 8 7 6 10 12 13 14
```

### Violation 1

```text id="f8q2n4"
8 > 7
```

Store:

```text id="r1m6k9"
first = 8
second = 7
```

---

### Violation 2

```text id="c5v8t3"
7 > 6
```

Update:

```text id="b2x9w6"
second = 6
```

---

### Final Swap

```text id="m7q4p1"
swap(8, 6)
```

Recovered BST:

```text id="t8k3r5"
4 5 6 7 8 10 12 13 14
```

# Final Code Logic

```cpp id="d9v2x7"
if(parent && root->data < parent->data){

    if(swappedNodes.first)
        swappedNodes.second = root;

    else{
        swappedNodes.first = parent;
        swappedNodes.second = root;
    }
}
```

This correctly handles both adjacent and non-adjacent swaps.
